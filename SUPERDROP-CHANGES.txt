SUPER DROP — WHAT WE ADDED (one pull request per thing)

Only the finished features we built on top of the base app, plus a groundwork
section for the pieces we started but didn't finish. Names are written to sell
the feature; the plain description leads, with a short "Technical" block under
the ones that need it. (PR descriptions on GitHub are Markdown, so screenshots /
GIFs can be dragged straight in — good fit for the UI ones.)


====================================================================
FINISHED FEATURES
====================================================================

1) Bottom sheet for sending
   Sending from the share menu opens a bottom sheet instead of dragging you into
   the app. Shows icons for the devices you can tap to choose who to send to. No
   more being forced all the way into the app just to send or receive something —
   just like the old Quick Share. A much better improvement.

   Image for the PR: docs/pr-images/send-bottom-sheet.png

2) Quick tile that opens a bottom sheet to receive
   A Quick Settings tile that opens the receive sheet and makes you visible to
   any nearby phone, so they can send to you straight away — no opening the app
   and digging for a button. Tapping the tile also turns on Wi-Fi and Bluetooth
   for you (and restores them when you close the sheet) and closes the
   notification shade so you can see your screen.

   Technical: a TileService whose tap forces the receiver "visible" override on,
   starts the foreground receiver service with ACTION_TILE_WAKE (which calls the
   radio-helper to enable Wi-Fi + BT), and uses startActivityAndCollapse to drop
   the shade. The prior visibility + radio state is captured and restored when the
   receive sheet closes.

   Image for the PR: docs/pr-images/receive-tile-sheet.png

3) Tap to share
   You can now tap two phones together to start a transfer. The tap passes your
   identity and your Wi-Fi address and starts the transfer. It even works when the
   other phone is just sitting idle with the app closed — the tap wakes it into
   receive mode — and a single tap is all it takes.

   Technical: uses standard Android NFC. The receiver runs a Host Card Emulation
   service on the Nearby Connections AID F00000FE2C; the sender uses NFC
   reader-mode. On tap the sender sends SELECT + an ADVERTISEMENT command and the
   receiver answers with its endpoint info plus a Wi-Fi-LAN address (IP:port)
   packed in Quick Share's own NFC blob (the "deym" tag, marked
   P2P_POINT_TO_POINT). NFC only does the handoff — the actual file transfer then
   runs over the normal Wi-Fi (mDNS/TCP) path. "Idle phone" support: tapping a
   closed/idle receiver's HCE starts its foreground service, opens a short
   visibility window, and posts the Accept prompt — Android can launch an HCE
   service even when the app's process is dead, which is what makes the cold tap
   work.

   Real-device findings (2026-06-09) and what we did about each:

   • RECEIVE into our app, on Android 15, was being stolen by Google.
     Why (verified from Google's code + on device): when two apps register the
     same NFC ID, Android 15 routes the tap to the default "wallet" app (Google),
     so our app never saw it (on Android 14 it showed a chooser instead). Fix
     added: while our receive sheet is open we now claim the NFC ID for the tap
     (CardEmulation.setPreferredService in ConsentTrampolineActivity), the one
     documented way to win it on Android 15. Sheet closed → it falls back to
     native. (Built; on-device confirmation pending.)

   • SEND from our app to a receiver, by tap, didn't start the transfer.
     Why (verified from a real device log + Google's code): a receiver that's
     idle answers the tap empty and only THEN wakes itself into receive and
     starts advertising — so the single NFC read got nothing back. Fix added
     (this is how stock Quick Share itself behaves): the tap is now a one-shot
     WAKE, not a blocking re-poll. On an empty answer we treat the tap as having
     woken the receiver, open a short window, and let our existing (working) Wi-Fi
     discovery find the now-advertising receiver and auto-connect into the normal
     transfer path. It re-arms on every tap (fixes "the second tap did nothing").
     CONFIRMED WORKING ON DEVICE — a tap now starts the transfer.

   • To see what's happening without a PC: added on-device diagnostics that
     auto-upload the NFC tap details, since the tester has no adb. They now
     upload RELIABLY — during a share the phone's only network is the local
     share Wi-Fi (no internet), so a POST sent mid-share was silently dropped;
     the uploader now queues each report and sends the whole queue the moment a
     real-internet network is back (or instantly over cellular). Added a Settings
     toggle, "Show NFC tap diagnostics" (on by default), to silence the on-screen
     messages once everything works.

   • A Bluetooth pairing request sometimes appeared during the tap.
     Verified it is NOT coming from our transfer: every socket our connection
     opens is insecure (no pairing) and our Bluetooth-Classic route is unused.
     The prompt originates outside our connect path (e.g. an OEM NFC→Bluetooth
     handover, or the other phone's own bootstrap); exact source not yet pinned
     (needs: which phone shows it + the device name on the prompt).

   Honest status: SEND-by-tap is CONFIRMED WORKING on a phone — a tap now starts
   the transfer (one-shot wake → our existing Wi-Fi transfer). The ONE remaining
   difference from stock Quick Share: the receiver shows an Accept prompt before
   the transfer starts, whereas Quick-Share-to-Quick-Share starts with no prompt.
   That "no prompt" is Google's self-share auto-accept, which only fires between
   two devices on the SAME signed-in Google account — an account-bound trust check
   we can't forge for a different account, so for now the receiver taps Accept
   once. The Android-15 RECEIVE fix above (claiming the NFC ID while our sheet is
   open) is built but still pending on-device confirmation. Full reverse-engineered
   byte map + root causes live in docs/NFC_INTEROP_BYTEMAP.md; device-test notes
   in BUGS.md #6.

4) Tap an iPhone to share a link
   Tap your phone with an iPhone (or any phone) and it opens your pairing link in
   that phone's browser — a simple way to hand off to a device that doesn't run
   the app.

   Technical: the phone exposes an NDEF record (an HCE NDEF service) carrying the
   QR pairing URL. iOS reads NDEF tags without an app and offers to open the URL,
   so the iPhone lands on the pairing link in Safari. Separate from #3 — this is
   a plain NDEF URL tag, not the Quick Share AID exchange.

5) Made the heads-up notification prettier
   Redesigned the incoming-transfer pop-up so the Accept and Decline buttons are
   bigger, clearer, and easier to hit — color-coded and on opposite sides, with a
   thumbnail of what's coming in, so you can act on it at a glance.

   Technical: a custom RemoteViews notification layout under
   DecoratedCustomViewStyle (Decline left, Accept right) with a drawn placeholder
   thumbnail and a tinted small icon.

   Image for the PR: docs/pr-images/receive-notification.jpg

6) "Files received" notification with an Open button
   When a transfer finishes you get a heads-up that opens the file you just
   received in one tap (or your Downloads if it can't open it directly).

   Technical: a high-importance completion notification; the Open action resolves
   the received file's MediaStore Downloads content:// Uri (ACTION_VIEW with a
   read grant), falling back to the system Downloads view when the single file
   can't be resolved.

7) Automatically turn Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on for a transfer, then off again
   When a transfer starts, the app switches Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on for you and
   then puts them back exactly how they were once it's done, so you never have to
   fiddle with your radios. Set up once and it keeps working after every restart.
   (Requires a one-time wireless-debugging / ADB setup.)
   Ships with a small companion helper app that does the actual switching —
   needed because the main app can't toggle the radios on modern Android by
   itself; the helper handles it and the main app just asks it to.

   Technical:
   - Why a separate helper: the radio-toggle APIs are gated by targetSdk, not by
     a grantable permission. WifiManager.setWifiEnabled() only works for an app
     targeting SDK <= 28, and BluetoothAdapter.enable()/disable() only for SDK
     <= 32. The main app targets a modern SDK, so it legally can't call them; the
     helper is a tiny APK (dev.superdrop.radiohelper) that targets SDK 28 and can.
   - How apps talk to it: the helper exposes a bound Messenger service guarded by
     a signature permission (BIND_RADIO). Clients need the same signing key and a
     <queries> entry for the helper package. Two modes: SESSION
     (prepareForShare / transferFinished — the helper captures the original radio
     state, turns on only what was off, and restores only what it turned on) and
     DIRECT (setWifi / setBluetooth / queryState).
   - Bluetooth: zero setup — the SDK-28 helper calls BluetoothAdapter.enable()/
     disable() directly.
   - Wi-Fi (silent): needs WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS, which a sideloaded app can only
     get over ADB. The helper does this once, on-device, with no PC needed after
     setup:
       * pair to the phone's own wireless debugging (Android 11+) via a
         notification inline-reply (so the pairing dialog stays open), then
       * self-grant the permission:
           adb shell pm grant dev.superdrop.radiohelper android.permission.WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS
         (run once; survives reboot, lost only on reinstall), then
       * toggle Wi-Fi over the local ADB connection with:
           svc wifi enable   /   svc wifi disable
   - No per-reboot step: on boot the helper re-enables wireless debugging
     (adb_wifi_enabled via WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS), re-finds the local adbd port
     over mDNS (_adb-tls-connect._tcp), and reconnects with its stored key.
   - Fallback ladder if a silent path isn't available on the device: direct
     setWifiEnabled -> self-ADB -> Shizuku -> the Wi-Fi settings panel.

8) Battery-optimization button that actually works on OnePlus
   The built-in "stop the app being put to sleep" button didn't work on OnePlus,
   so we replaced it with a one-tap Allow that sticks (and falls back to the
   settings list on phones that ignore the pop-up).

   Technical: fires ACTION_REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS (the one-tap
   "Allow" dialog) and, on OEM ROMs that no-op it, escalates to the App Info /
   battery settings page.
